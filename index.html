About the site

A Maya city in the forest

Caracol is a major ancient Maya archaeological site in Belize's Cayo District. It is located within the Chiquibul Forest Reserve, about 50 miles from San Ignacio Town.

The site covers about 25,000 acres. At its height, the ancient city is estimated to have covered approximately 38 square miles and supported a population of about 80,000–100,000 people.

Its most famous structure is Caana, meaning “Sky Place.” At about 139 feet, it is the tallest building in Belize according to the National Institute of Culture and History (NICH).

A recent archaeological discovery

Caracol continues to reveal its history

During the January 2025 field season, archaeologists working with the Institute of Archaeology reported uncovering the final resting place of Caracol's first ruler, Te K'ab Chaak. Discoveries like this help researchers understand more about the ancient city's rulers and development.
